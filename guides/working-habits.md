# Working habits

A standing list of how to work, for any game, not only this one. Keep it general. Project-specific notes, gaps and
TODOs go in the project, not here.

Source: a video on game-dev productivity tips the user shared on 2026-09-30 (title not recorded).

1. **Work to a hard deadline, like a jam.** "When it's finished" never comes. A fixed date keeps the scope small
   and gets the game over the line. Cut features, never move the date.
2. **Build debug tools early.** Teleporting, skipping scenes, starting from a chosen state, global debug switches.
   They're dev-only and never ship, but they save hours of testing.
3. **Write distractions down, don't chase them.** A small bug or an optimisation spotted mid-task gets one line in
   the notes, and the current task goes on. "Only ten minutes" is how rabbit holes start.
4. **Learn your tools' hotkeys.** Look them up, print them, drill them. The time pays back on every working
   minute of every project.
5. **Do favours for your future self.** Spend the last five minutes of a session writing exactly what the next one
   starts with. Or stop in the middle of a feature, so it's easy to pick up again.
6. **Solve small recurring annoyances for good.** Slow builds, loud previews, a tool that needs restarting every
   morning: script or automate them once. Small fixes pay back every day.
7. **Make the workspace easy to sit down at.** Keep it clean and used only for work. Buy good versions of what you
   use daily (chair, water bottle), and keep things around you that you enjoy.
8. **Find the right people.** Game dev is lonely. Honest, encouraging people, locally or online, keep you going.
