# Funding channels and link rules

Scope: where a solo web-game dev can ask players for support (Kickstarter, Patreon and friends, Steam), and whether each web platform lets a free "lite" build ask for it.

Researched 2026-09-30. Quotes are verbatim from the linked pages. "Unverified" marks claims I could not confirm on a primary page.

## TL;DR

1. **The big portals do not let a game ask for money elsewhere.** YouTube Playables, GameDistribution and Playgama's GameDistribution route forbid outgoing links. Poki says its ads are "the only form of monetization". CrazyGames allows a Steam link (desktop, main menu or end of demo) but bans cross-promotion and needs consent to promote your own products.
2. **A Steam link is the only call to action that portals partly accept.** Patreon or Kickstarter buttons inside a portal build are not allowed or not covered anywhere. Assume "no" until the portal says yes in writing.
3. **Your own page and itch.io are where the ask lives.** itch.io has built-in Patreon access and pay-what-you-want. Newgrounds has no rule against author links that I could find (unverified). Both are fine homes for the "support the full game" build.
4. **Wishlists are the currency.** The median game sells about 0.15x its launch wishlists in week one (0.10x above $10). Steam emails wishlisters at launch and on discounts. A Steam "Coming Soon" page costs $100 and is the cheapest durable ask.
5. **Kickstarter needs an audience you already have.** About 32% of video game campaigns succeed. Kickstarter brings roughly 3–4 backers for every 7 you bring. Advisers say: release-quality footage, a demo, 500+ followers, 3 months of pre-launch.
6. **Patreon is side income, not funding, for most game devs.** Games creators on Patreon average under $100/month (my arithmetic on Graphtreon totals), and the top earners are map makers and modders, not indie game devs. Patreon now takes 10% plus processing for new creators.
7. **Keep rewards digital and cheap.** Early builds, devlogs, votes, a name in credits. Physical rewards and per-patron work eat the dev time the money was meant to buy.
8. **Promise the fragment, not the future.** Say what exists, what is planned, and that plans can change. Kickstarter makes you refund or explain if you cannot deliver; about 12% of funded video game projects did not deliver (2015 study).
9. **Let platforms handle tax.** Steam, Patreon and itch.io ("collected by itch.io") collect VAT for you. Kickstarter's pledge manager can too. Direct tips and your own shop mean you handle it.
10. **Smallest thing that works:** a Steam page, a plain "wishlist on Steam" line where allowed, one itch.io page with the ask. Add Patreon or Kickstarter only after people are asking how to pay.

## 1. Kickstarter for small and solo video games

### Numbers

- 2025: games raised about $26M on Kickstarter, level with 2024, with 443 funded video game projects ([The Game Business](https://www.thegamebusiness.com/p/why-arent-more-game-developers-using), citing ICO Partners). 2024 was a record count: "441 successful video game campaigns—a 9% increase from 2023" ([Kickstarter](https://updates.kickstarter.com/kickstarter-biggest-platform-for-games/)).
- Success rate for video game campaigns: "Overall success rate: 32%" ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/kickstarter-in-2025-and-before)). Compare tabletop at 80% in 2024.
- Average successful campaign: about $58,000 in 2025 ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-worked-for-video-game-crowdfunding)). The average is pulled up by a few big ones: 11 campaigns passed $500k in 2025 ([The Game Business](https://www.thegamebusiness.com/p/why-arent-more-game-developers-using)). I found no published median for video games (unverified; expect it to be far lower).
- Money is concentrated in mid-size goals: "68.1% [of funds] from campaigns under $250K" and "None of the 15 campaigns aiming to raise $2.5 million or more was successful" ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/kickstarter-in-2025-and-before)).
- Fees: 5% Kickstarter fee plus about 3% + $0.20 per pledge processing, charged only if funded ([Kickstarter fee guide](https://updates.kickstarter.com/kickstarter-fees-a-comprehensive-guide-for-creators/), summary via search; the help page returned 403).

### "Kickstarter needs an existing audience"

- Kickstarter's own games lead, quoted: "For every seven backers that you bring to the platform, Kickstarter will bring you three to four." ([The Game Business](https://www.thegamebusiness.com/p/why-arent-more-game-developers-using)). So about 70% of backers are yours.
- Thomas Bidaux (ICO Partners) lists three red flags for video game Kickstarters: children's games, mobile games, and free-to-play ([The Game Business](https://www.thegamebusiness.com/p/why-arent-more-game-developers-using)). A free web game is close to the last flag: backers must want a paid, bigger thing.
- Prep cost: about three months before launch plus four months of campaign and follow-up work (same source).
- ICO Partners' 2025 review says campaigns now need release-quality footage, ideally a demo, and an engaged audience; one adviser says not to launch under 500 followers ([GameDev.net summary](https://gamedev.net/news/6014-crowdfunding-is-fundamentally-a-marketing-and-a-community-exercise-what/); page returned 403, taken from search summary, so unverified wording). The original ICO post also returned 403: [Medium](https://medium.com/icopartners/kickstarter-and-video-games-in-2025-90f15c2fd7bd).
- Pre-launch "Coming Soon" followers convert to backers at roughly 20–30% ([Prelaunch Club](https://prelaunch.marketing/blogs/academy/average-conversion-rates-for-kickstarter-followers), mostly tabletop data; unverified for video games).
- What worked in 2025: demos handed to creators and press (one game raised $157k on a $47k goal that way), and Coming Soon pages. Bidaux: "the practice of launching a Coming Soon page is more and more common" ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/what-worked-for-video-game-crowdfunding)).

### Steam wishlists plus Kickstarter

- Common advice is a few hundred email subscribers and a few thousand Steam wishlists before launching a campaign ([The Game Marketer](https://www.thegamemarketer.com/insight-posts/how-to-run-a-successful-kickstarter-for-your-indie-game), via search summary; unverified).
- Steam keys as rewards are allowed only if the game already has a Steam App ID: "Steam Keys can only be promoted as a reward for products that have already confirmed Steam distribution." And: "The maximum duration of time-limited crowdfunding is 30 days. Steam keys are not intended to be used for ongoing crowdfunding ('slacker backer' or Late Pledge campaigns)" ([Steamworks: Keys](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/keys)). Beta keys for backers are capped at "generally 2,500 total".
- So the order is: Steam page first, then Kickstarter. The Steam page gathers wishlists before, during and after the campaign.
- Backers who get keys do not buy on Steam at launch, so they do not count toward launch sales (a known trade-off, [The Game Business](https://www.thegamebusiness.com/p/why-arent-more-game-developers-using)).

### Other platforms

- **BackerKit** began as a pledge manager and has run crowdfunding since 2022; **Gamefound** takes 5% plus processing and bought Indiegogo in July 2025 ([LaunchBoom](https://www.launchboom.com/crowdfunding-guides/kickstarter-vs-gamefound-vs-backerkit/), [Gamefound fees](https://gamefound.com/en/info/fees)). Both lean tabletop. Kickstarter is still where video game backers are.
- **Fig** (equity-style game crowdfunding) was dissolved into Republic in May 2023 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fig_(company)), [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/crowdfunding-website-fig-is-no-more/)). Lesson: niche platforms come and go; do not build a plan around one.

**Verdict: later.** Only after a Steam page with a few thousand wishlists, a demo, and a mailing list or Discord of hundreds. Probably once in the whole strategy, for the "real game", not per fragment.

## 2. Patreon, Ko-fi, itch.io, GitHub Sponsors

### What game devs earn

- Graphtreon tracks 21,113 games creators with at least one paying member and about $2.03M/month in total ([Graphtreon games stats](https://graphtreon.com/patreon-stats/games)). That is an average under $100/month (my arithmetic), and the median is surely lower.
- The top 10 in Graphtreon's games category are tabletop map makers, VTT module makers and similar, not indie video game devs ([Graphtreon top earners](https://graphtreon.com/top-patreon-earners/games)). Adult games have their own ranking.
- A practitioner summary without data: "Starting from zero followers, Patreon is a landing page with no traffic." and "a few hundred dollars monthly won't fund development, but it pays for the capsule artist, the sound library, the localization" ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/patreon-for-game-developers-is-it-worth-it); opinion, no data).

### What works

- Cheap-to-fulfil tiers: "early builds (you ship them anyway), devlog access, polls, your name scrolled in credits by a script" (Bugnet, above).
- Fails when tiers add up to "a part-time job": monthly custom content, credit admin, Discord duties, per-patron attention (same source).
- Needs a steady visible stream of progress, or patrons churn. A 4–8 month cycle of public fragments fits this well: each fragment is an update.

### Fees and tax

- Patreon: new creators after August 4, 2025 pay "a standard platform rate of 10% on all earnings plus applicable fees and taxes" ([Patreon help](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/36426991446797-A-standard-platform-fee-for-new-creators-effective-after-August-4-2025), via search; page returned 403). Processing is about 2.9% + $0.30 per payment (search summaries; unverified on primary).
- Patreon collects and remits VAT in most places; in some countries it shares the duty with you ([Patreon: How VAT works](https://support.patreon.com/hc/en-us/articles/205259549-How-VAT-works-for-creators-on-Patreon), via search).
- Ko-fi: 0% platform fee on one-off tips, 5% on memberships and shop on the free plan, 0% with Gold ([Ko-fi pricing](https://ko-fi.com/pricing); page returned 403, figures from search results, and one source says new sellers now pay 5% on tips too, so unverified). You are the seller, so tax is yours.
- itch.io: "open revenue sharing", default 10%, creator can change it. Pay-what-you-want with a minimum "which can be 0". "EU VAT ... compliance is automatic when using Collected by itch.io, paid later" ([itch.io creator FAQ](https://itch.io/docs/creators/faq)).
- itch.io + Patreon: "itch.io's Patreon integration allows you to link your Patreon campaign to your itch.io project page, enabling your patrons to claim access to your itch.io project" ([itch.io docs](https://itch.io/docs/creators/patreon)). This is the cheapest way to deliver early builds to patrons.
- GitHub Sponsors: 0% fee from personal accounts, but eligibility is for open source contributors ([GitHub docs](https://docs.github.com/en/sponsors/getting-started-with-github-sponsors/about-github-sponsors)). Only relevant if the game or its tools are open source.

**Verdict:**
- itch.io page with pay-what-you-want: **must** (it is also the web home of the fragment).
- Ko-fi tip link: **worth it** only as a zero-effort "tip jar" on your own page. No tiers.
- Patreon: **later**, when people ask how to support you. Start with one tier: early builds via itch.io + devlog.
- GitHub Sponsors: **skip** unless you open-source.

## 3. Steam

### Wishlists

- GameDiscoverCo, games released Sep 2024–Aug 2025 with over 25k wishlists: median first-week sales 0.15x wishlists; above $10 the median drops to 0.10x. Spread is "10-20x, not 10-20%" ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-state-of-steam-wishlist-conversions)).
- Wishlists do not go stale: "There is no evidence that wishlists 'get old.'" Song of Iron converted 8.02% on average across cohorts ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/01/27/do-wishlists-get-old/)). So a Steam page opened early is not wasted.
- Why they matter: the algorithm reads wishlists in "Popular Upcoming" and the "Discovery Queue". The bigger effect is the email: at launch or discount "everyone who wishlisted it gets an email. And the email triggers sales and money earned triggers the algorithm." There is "no magic number" ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/09/04/killing-the-myths-behind-steams-visibility/)). Common target: 7,000–10,000 before launch (same site, via search).
- Steam Direct fee is $100 per app, recoupable after $1,000 gross ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/appfee), via search).

### Demos

- "Demos are a separate App ID that is associated with your full game's App ID." Free. "Steam will automatically include a prominent link from your demo store page back to your full game". Demos get their own user reviews. A demo is "a small playable portion of your game that show some of the core mechanics", and can ship before the game ([Steamworks: Demos](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/application/demos)).
- A web fragment can later become the Steam demo if it is really a slice of the full game.

### Links to and from Steam

- Linking to your Steam page from anywhere you control is normal practice; nothing in Steamworks forbids it.
- On the store page: "You cannot use your store page descriptions or graphical assets to promote or link to other websites. This includes via embedded images, QR codes and even implied URLs via text such as 'DOTA2 DOT COM'." Use the link fields instead ([Steamworks: Description](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/page/description)).
- Link fields cover your website plus YouTube, X, Facebook, Twitch and Discord ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/steam-now-supports-social-media-links-on-store-pages), 2019; Discord from search results). No Patreon or Kickstarter field that I found. So a Kickstarter or Patreon link goes on your website, which the website field points to. Mentioning "funded on Kickstarter" in plain text is common; a URL is not allowed (inference from the rule above).
- Patreon rewards that include Steam keys: the keys rule allows keys only for time-limited (max 30 days) crowdfunding, and not for ongoing campaigns before release (see section 1). A monthly Patreon is ongoing, so do not promise Steam keys to patrons before launch. Use itch.io builds or a Steam Playtest instead (inference; ask Valve if it matters).

**Verdict: must.** Open the Steam page as soon as the real game has a look. It is the one ask every channel can point to.

### What this means

- **The strategy and the portals pull in opposite directions.** Portals pay for attention with ads and want players to stay. A fragment whose job is to send players to Patreon, Kickstarter or Steam breaks most portal rules.
- **Exclusivity blocks the "same fragment everywhere" idea.** Poki's web-exclusive deal would stop the same build going on itch.io or Newgrounds. GameDistribution requires the build to be identical everywhere, so a portal-clean build and a "with ask" build cannot both live there.
- **Two builds is the workable pattern:** a portal build with no ask (or only CrazyGames' Steam link), and a home build (own site, itch.io, Newgrounds) with the ask. Portal traffic still builds name recognition; the studio logo on the loading screen is allowed on Poki.
- **Portal descriptions** are platform metadata, not the game. Rules there differ and I did not verify them per portal. CrazyGames points app store links to "configurable game metadata fields".

## 5. Legal and ops basics

- **Tax.** Steam: "Steam pricing is VAT inclusive ... Valve remits the tax collected" but "You will need to consult with your tax advisors on your own tax obligations" ([Steamworks tax FAQ](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/finance/taxfaq)). Patreon collects VAT in most cases. itch.io handles EU VAT in "collected by itch.io" mode, not in direct mode. Kickstarter's pledge manager can collect sales tax and VAT, or you opt out and handle it yourself ([Kickstarter pledge manager tax FAQ](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/38567345467675-Pledge-Manager-Tax-FAQ), via search). Portal payouts are income; CrazyGames says "Payments are inclusive of VAT". Income tax is always yours.
- **Fulfilment risk.** Mollick's study (projects 2009–2015): about 9% of funded Kickstarters failed to deliver, about 12% for video games; campaigns under $1,000 were most likely to fail ([MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/12-of-video-game-kickstarter-campaigns-fail-to-deliver/), [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2699251)). Physical rewards (shirts, boxes) add shipping, customs and VAT per country. Keep all rewards digital.
- **If you cannot finish.** Kickstarter's terms require creators to fulfil or refund; a creator who cannot finish must post what was done, show how funds were used, act "in good faith", and offer remaining funds back ([Kickstarter help](https://help.kickstarter.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005028834-What-is-a-creator-obligated-to-do-once-their-project-is-funded), via search; page returned 403).
- **Framing fragments honestly.**
  - Label the build: "A free fragment of [game], a work in progress. This is not the full game."
  - Say what exists now, what is planned, and that plans can change.
  - Sell what already exists (early builds, the current fragment), not a promised feature list.
  - On Patreon, pledges pay for ongoing work, not a finished product. Say so in the tier text.
  - YouTube Playables also bans metadata that tricks users "into believing the Playables content is something it is not" ([Trust and safety](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/certification/requirements_trustsafety)). A fragment label protects you there too.

## Channel weighing

| Channel | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Steam Coming Soon page | **Must** | $100, durable, every other channel can point to it, wishlists convert and do not expire |
| itch.io page (web build, pay-what-you-want) | **Must** | Allows the ask, handles VAT, has Patreon integration |
| Own site page with the ask | **Worth it** | One static page; Steam's website field points to it |
| Portals with a clean build | **Worth it** for reach, not for the ask | Ads and name recognition; accept no in-game ask |
| CrazyGames Steam link (desktop, menu or end) | **Worth it** | The only portal ask that is written into the rules |
| Newgrounds | **Worth it** | Author links appear allowed (unverified); audience likes web games |
| Ko-fi tip link | **Later** | Zero-effort, but tips alone are small |
| Patreon | **Later** | Only when people ask; one tier; delivery via itch.io |
| Kickstarter | **Later**, once | Needs wishlists, demo, hundreds of followers, months of work |
| Poki web-exclusive deal | **Decide case by case** | Blocks the same build on itch.io and Newgrounds for years |
| BackerKit / Gamefound | **Skip** | Tabletop audiences; no advantage for a video game |
| GitHub Sponsors | **Skip** | Needs open source |
| Kongregate | **Skip** | Closed to new games since 2020 |

## What to do

1. Open a Steam page for the real game as soon as it has a stable look.
2. Build every fragment in two flavours from one codebase: a portal build with no ask, and a home build with a short end-screen ask ("Wishlist on Steam" first, "Support on Patreon" later).
3. For CrazyGames only, add the Steam link on the desktop main menu or end screen.
4. For Poki, ask in writing before adding any link; for GameDistribution, ask for consent or leave links out; for YouTube Playables, put links on the channel only.
5. Put the home build on itch.io with pay-what-you-want and the fragment label.
6. Add a Ko-fi or Patreon link only when players ask how to pay. Start Patreon with one digital tier.
7. Consider Kickstarter once, for the real game, after a few thousand wishlists and a demo.
8. Keep every reward digital. Never promise Steam keys to an ongoing Patreon before launch.
