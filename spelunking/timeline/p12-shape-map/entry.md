# p12 · Shape map (v9)

p11 left a beautiful toy: a cave ecosystem that can be pushed through tipping points. A toy is not yet a game, and
there was no answer to "what mechanics does this game need?". This entry is the design phase that found one. It
didn't build a prototype. It built a map: candidate shapes, reference games and the target audience on one picture of
gaming motivations, and the game's pillars placed on it. Each ▶ opens the map as it stood at a milestone, with the
working documents of that moment beside it.

## Question

Which game goes around the toy, matched to an audience that will be thrilled by it?

**Pass:** a few distinct pillars that sit around the audience's motivations, swing between thinking and doing, and
follow the rules collected in [build-principles](builds/v9.6/build-principles.md).

**Kill:** the shapes scatter with no audience to hold them together, and the choice falls back on taste.

## Assumptions

1. [✓] Candidate shapes and reference games can be compared on one motivation map, so the shape is placed instead of picked by taste. Held: the pillars were placed on the map around the audience (v9.4). (post-hoc)
2. [✓] Successful small indies space their core and supporting loops apart, a swing between pillars that lets the player rest from one in the other. Held as likely: six of nine reference games show it; Stacklands and SUMMERHOUSE don't ([findings](builds/v9.6/findings.md)). (post-hoc)
3. [✓] The factors the audience decides stay open until the audience is chosen. Held: the audience mix came first (v9.3), the pillars after it (v9.4). (post-hoc)
4. [?] The three pillars weigh the same for this audience. Nothing has been played yet. (post-hoc)
5. [?] The lifecycle's rules read as natural and need no text: five stages, three decisions, the rest on autopilot. (post-hoc)

## Limitations

- [constraint ?] Every position on the map is a judgement (the shapes', the reference games', the pillars'), not measured data. The user checked the games they had played.
- [cut] The market part of stage 4 (competition and success rate per market) is not done; it is the next step.
- [cut] Stage 5, loop times and complexity, is parked until the end, as the method says.
- [cut] Nothing playable: the first prototype of the pillars comes after this.

## Built

**The method** (user, 2026-10-02), in [the working folder](../../../concepts/shape-exploration/README.md):
broaden (29 shapes), deepen, consolidate (two anchors), assess (each shape's motivation mix on Quantic Foundry's map),
then loop times. The map page is the assessment drawn: every shape on two axes, World ↔ Player and Cerebral ↔ Kinetic.

**The milestones**, each frozen with the folder as it was:

| Build | What it added |
|---|---|
| v9.1 | The pool of 29 shapes, each with the reason for its place |
| v9.2 | Reference games split into their loops, with each audience read from its Steam tags; the finding that core and supporting loops swing between pillars |
| v9.3 | Loops draggable when opened from disk; a card for each motivation; this game's audience mix (user) |
| v9.4 | Three equal pillars around the audience; [build-principles](builds/v9.4/build-principles.md) starts: accessible and never boring, through systems |
| v9.5 | The taming direction: an ecology theme with no technology, pets that go on excursions, pillars named, flows between them |
| v9.6 | Every interaction as one arrow with an action, a condition and an outcome; the [lifecycle](builds/v9.6/lifecycle.html) of terrain, flora and fauna, with who does each step |

**Where it ended** ([build-principles](builds/v9.6/build-principles.md)): ground moves Unseen → Wild → Caverns →
Cultivated. The pioneers open the Unseen and tame fauna up the food chain to the top predator. The foragers study and
forage, and build a colony once the top predator is tamed. A colony harvests itself. Only those three decisions carry
a name; the hands-on game around them is levelling and combining the pet teams.

**The game shape** (2026-10-04, [build-principles](../../../concepts/shape-exploration/build-principles.md) →
*Game shape*): an incremental idle game. A session is one or more runs, and a run is anything that pushes the needle;
colonies, foragers and scouts keep working while the player is away. Long-term progression climbs differently, not
faster (D188, D189).
