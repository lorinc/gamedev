# Brief: what is given

Supporting document for the [shape exploration](README.md). Everything here is the user's (2026-10-02), quoted with
spelling lightly fixed. These are the inputs the exercise starts from; the stages don't reopen them.

## What is kept: the toy

> "A cave ecosystem simulation that can be pushed through a series of point-of-no-return tipping points, or at least
> locally stable optimums. This is what the implemented game already has. I love the visual nature of it, I love that
> it is procgen, I love that the simulator itself is also a tool that helps balancing the game, and I love the
> potential extensions of it, like having different climates/microbiomes in isolated caverns. It's an awesome,
> beautiful toy. And note the TOY word here, a toy is not yet a game."

## Fixed by the user (not left to the audience)

- **Peaceful.** "No problems and tension will be solved by violence. That's a game design limitation I choose to
  follow." Also: "I'm not sure I personally enjoy high-tension, high-action situations."
- **Surprise comes from discovery.** "OMG, what the HELL is going on in that cavern???! How did that happen? What are
  the implications? Is it useful? Dangerous? Can I replicate it?" The tipping points are an inherent climax mechanism
  (action → simulation → effect). Where a tipping point happens is open: it "can be isolated to a cavern in the cave
  system or to a lab in the player base".
- **Casual during play, with moments to stop and think.** "Relatively casual during gameplay, with elements of 'okay,
  let's stop and think / tinker for a moment here'. Noita does it very well."
- **A constrained loadout at all times.** "I'd love if the player had a 'loadout' at all times, and it had
  constraints, so it's not a hoarding game. Deck building is a good example for that."
- **A character and a base only for long-term emotional connection** (an opinion, stated as one). "I'd only add a
  char to this game for improving long-term emotional connection. The same applies to base building. A character and
  a base make it very personal, and I think that's a very good thing." A character can range from "practically a
  walking cursor" to "the centre of the game progress".
- **No pixel-based or tile-based digging.** "The cave system is WFC, so we can just go 'add wall here, make opening
  there', and it would work, but I definitely do not want pixel or tile-based digging."

## The fantasy in one sentence

> "Build a massive, diverse, fricking beautiful ecosystem that also functions as an idle factory."

## The user's observation on Noita

> "Noita is awesome, because it is so many things at once. It is a deck building game. It is an action-platformer. It
> is an alchemy game. It is an open world roguelike (not lite, there's no meta-progression). If you think of this,
> this is the tension-release cycle, but not in difficulty, but viable choices. Sometimes you just shoot with your
> loadout, but sometimes you stop and design it."

So the cycle the user wants is in the count of viable choices: few while playing with the loadout, many while
designing it.

## Trophies

What a player ends up with; "depends on what we build":

- "a beautiful, isolated cavern that lives in a rare, delicate balance"
- "an awesome base"
- "a deck with massive, unusual synergy that clears an otherwise hard level with (ch)ease"

## The "silly" ones

- "tamed animals are cute"
- "rare trophies are nice"
- "I love when sometimes I can build, from the input random, applying deep game knowledge, absurdly powerful stuff."
  What "absurdly powerful" means depends on the shape: "If this was a CCG or deck builder, a cool combo would be one.
  If this is a farming game, flooding the cave temporarily to harvest everything is a power move."

## Tension

An example the user gave: "Can I increase the humidity level high enough before the natural next fire event happens?"
The user expects tension to "drop out from the genre intersection we play with".
