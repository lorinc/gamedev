# Idea: taming and the breach

Part of the [shape exploration](README.md). An idea, not a decision: a pattern from Sonny's fights, made general, and
how it could shape taming and the breach of the Unseen.

## The pattern: promote a side effect

Sonny (Krin, 2008 and 2010) is remembered for fights where every new area used something you already had in an
unexpected way. The mechanics behind it, made independent of fighting:

1. **A few shared dials.** Both sides run on the same small set of states. In Sonny: focus, buffs, debuffs, stuns,
   shields. The opponent needs these dials too.
2. **Every action has a main job and side effects on the dials.** The main job is the obvious one (damage). The side
   effects (drain focus, stun, dispel) look like extras.
3. **A challenge runs on one dial.** Each boss is built so its threat depends on a dial the player can already
   touch, but only through a side effect. Bosses that heal from focus are beaten by draining it; a sniper who
   charges a one-shot kill for two turns is beaten by a stun in that window.
4. **The answer is a promotion.** The key to the challenge is a side effect the player has owned since an earlier
   area, now used as the main job. That's where the "unexpected, really smart" feeling comes from: the tool was
   always in the kit.
5. **The opponent telegraphs.** Its next action shows before it lands (two turns of charging), so the player
   adjusts the plan to it, and the outcome is strategy, not luck. Slay the Spire's intents, which shook the industry
   years later, are the same move.
6. **Rebuilding is expected.** The kit is chosen from a larger pool (an 8-slot wheel), and changing it for one
   challenge is cheap. The puzzle is choosing, not grinding.
7. **New dials, now and then.** A couple of areas in, a new dial unlocks and changes what the old tools mean.
8. **One lie, once trust exists.** Once the player reads the dials, one challenge shows a signal that means the
   opposite (a buff that claims weakness and hides strength). It teaches reading over memorising.
9. **Short enough to hold.** Every action is explainable in two or three lines.

**As a recipe:** dials D; each action = a main job plus side effects on D; each challenge = a threat that runs on
one dial d in D, with a windup; the design check = the answer to every challenge is a side effect on d that the
player already has, and it was never that action's main job.

## Applied to taming

- **The dials:** the animal's suspicion, its pickiness about food, its intrigue, and the team's endurance under the
  area's threats.
- **The actions:** pets' abilities, each with a main job and side effects on those dials. A healer's song might also
  lower suspicion; a burrower's tunnel might also bring live prey within reach.
- **The challenges:** each top predator runs on one dial. One is suspicious beyond anything food can fix; one eats
  only live prey; one comes only to a mating dance.
- **The promotion:** the key is a pet from an earlier biome whose side effect is that dial. This is the carry-over
  between biomes, through team synergy instead of a bait graph.
- **The telegraph:** the animal shows what it will do next (it circles, it sniffs and leaves, it watches from a
  distance), with no text, in line with "causality without walls of text". The player answers that, so a taming is
  won by reading, not by luck.
- **The lie:** a late animal that looks calm while it is the most suspicious of all.

## The breach as a scouting game

The breach could be its own game: the pioneers wander the Unseen until every entity that lives there has been
spotted. Not a fight and not a taming, but scouting.

- It fits "mysteries are visuals only": spotting is the reveal, and an area turns Wild when nothing in it is unseen.
- The same pattern applies. The dials are what hides an entity: camouflage, shyness, burrowing, being active only in
  the dark or at one time. The actions are pets' side effects: light, scent, noise, digging. Each Unseen has one
  entity that is hard to spot in one way, and an ability the player already owns spots it.
- Scouting, taming and building a colony then become three different kinds of play: wandering, a negotiation, and
  building.
